The morning light slipped through the curtains before I fully opened my eyes.

Warm.

Soft.

Familiar.

I reached out instinctively, expecting emptiness—

—but my hand brushed against something warm beside me.

Sora shifted slightly.

“…Haru,” she mumbled, half-asleep. “Stop stealing the blanket.”

“I’m not.”

“You are.”

“I live here too.”

“That’s irrelevant.”

Even with her eyes closed, she sounded completely convinced she was right.

I sighed and pulled the blanket back a little anyway.

She immediately grabbed it again.

We stayed like that for a moment, neither of us willing to lose a completely meaningless battle.

Then she finally opened one eye and looked at me.

“…Morning.”

“Morning.”

A pause.

Then she smiled a little.

“…You’re still here.”

I blinked.

“That’s usually how marriage works.”

“Good answer.”

She rolled over, closer this time, like it was the most natural thing in the world.

And somehow, it was.



The house was quiet after that.

Not empty.

Just calm.

Sora moved around the kitchen while I sat at the table, watching her without really thinking about it.

She noticed.

“Stop staring.”

“I’m not staring.”

“You are.”

“I’m observing.”

“That’s worse.”

She placed a plate in front of me.

Breakfast.

Simple.

Normal.

The kind of normal I never used to think about.

We ate in silence for a while.

Not uncomfortable.

Just… lived-in.

Like this was how life was always supposed to be.

At some point, Sora kicked my foot lightly under the table.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You kicked me.”

“I didn’t.”

“You did.”

“Prove it.”

I didn’t respond.

Because there was nothing to prove.

We both knew.



Later that day, we went out together.

Just walking.

No destination.

The city hadn’t changed much.

But I had.

People passed us without looking twice.

A couple arguing near a café.

Someone laughing on their phone.

Life moving normally around us.

Sora slipped her hand into mine without saying anything.

I looked at her.

She didn’t look back.

Like it was the most obvious thing in the world.

I tightened my grip slightly.

That was enough.



We stopped near a small park bench when her phone buzzed.

She checked it.

And for a moment… her expression changed.

Not fear.

Not surprise.

Something quieter.

“...Haru.”

“What is it?”

She turned the screen toward me.

A name I hadn’t heard in a long time.

Aoi.

I didn’t move for a second.

Just stared.

Sora watched me carefully.

Waiting.

I exhaled slowly.

“Is she here?”

“She’s asking to meet.”

Silence.

The wind moved through the trees above us.

It felt like the world had gotten quieter without asking permission.

I stood up.

Sora stood up with me immediately.

“No,” I said.

She paused.

Then I shook my head slightly.

“I mean… we’ll go.”

She studied my face.

“You don’t have to.”

“I know.”

Another pause.

Then I looked at her.

“I’m fine.”

Sora didn’t answer right away.

Then she squeezed my hand once.

Not tighter.

Just enough.

Like a reminder.



We found her near the station.

Aoi stood there like she wasn’t sure she was allowed to.

When she saw us, she froze.

“Haru…”

Her voice cracked slightly.

Sora didn’t let go of my hand.

She just stood beside me.

Quiet.

Present.

Aoi bowed her head almost immediately.

“I’m sorry.”

The words came fast.

Too fast.

Like she had been holding them in for years.

“I’m sorry for leaving. I’m sorry for everything. I didn’t know what to do back then, I—”

She stopped.

Her breathing shook.

“I messed everything up.”

Silence again.

Longer this time.

I looked at her.

And I realized something strange.

I wasn’t angry.

I wasn’t even hurt anymore.

It felt distant.

Like something that had happened to someone else.

“I know,” I said quietly.

Aoi flinched.

I shook my head slightly.

“I don’t hate you.”

That made her look up.

“But I’m not… there anymore.”

Her eyes widened a little.

Like she understood before I finished.

“I’ve moved on.”

Another pause.

Sora’s hand tightened just slightly in mine.

Not possessive.

Just there.

Aoi looked between us.

Really looked.

And something in her expression shifted.

Not jealousy.

Not regret anymore.

Just understanding.

“…I see,” she whispered.

Her shoulders dropped a little.

Like she had finally put something down she had been carrying for too long.

“I’m glad,” she said softly. “That you’re okay.”

It wasn’t a plea anymore.

Just honesty.

Sora gave a small nod.

Nothing dramatic.

Just acknowledgment.

Aoi stepped back.

Then again.

And then she turned away.

“I won’t bother you again.”

This time, there was no collapse in her voice.

Only acceptance.

We watched her leave.

Until she disappeared into the crowd.



For a while, neither of us moved.

The station noise returned slowly, like the world remembered how to breathe.

Sora finally spoke.

“That went better than I thought.”

“Yeah.”

She glanced at me.

“You okay?”

I thought about it.

Really thought about it.

Then I looked at her.

“I’m fine.”

A pause.

Then I added:

“Because I’ve got you.”

Sora didn’t answer immediately.

Then she leaned into me slightly, just enough that I could feel her there.

“…Good.”

We started walking home.

Hand in hand.

And for the first time, there was nothing left chasing either of us.
