<p>The morning light slipped through the curtains before I fully opened my eyes.</p>
<p>Warm.</p>
<p>Soft.</p>
<p>Familiar.</p>
<p>I reached out instinctively, expecting emptiness—</p>
<p>—but my hand brushed against something warm beside me.</p>
<p>Sora shifted slightly.</p>
<p>“…Haru,” she mumbled, half-asleep. “Stop stealing the blanket.”</p>
<p>“I’m not.”</p>
<p>“You are.”</p>
<p>“I live here too.”</p>
<p>“That’s irrelevant.”</p>
<p>Even with her eyes closed, she sounded completely convinced she was right.</p>
<p>I sighed and pulled the blanket back a little anyway.</p>
<p>She immediately grabbed it again.</p>
<p>We stayed like that for a moment, neither of us willing to lose a completely meaningless battle.</p>
<p>Then she finally opened one eye and looked at me.</p>
<p>“…Morning.”</p>
<p>“Morning.”</p>
<p>A pause.</p>
<p>Then she smiled a little.</p>
<p>“…You’re still here.”</p>
<p>I blinked.</p>
<p>“That’s usually how marriage works.”</p>
<p>“Good answer.”</p>
<p>She rolled over, closer this time, like it was the most natural thing in the world.</p>
<p>And somehow, it was.</p>
<p>The house was quiet after that.</p>
<p>Not empty.</p>
<p>Just calm.</p>
<p>Sora moved around the kitchen while I sat at the table, watching her without really thinking about it.</p>
<p>She noticed.</p>
<p>“Stop staring.”</p>
<p>“I’m not staring.”</p>
<p>“You are.”</p>
<p>“I’m observing.”</p>
<p>“That’s worse.”</p>
<p>She placed a plate in front of me.</p>
<p>Breakfast.</p>
<p>Simple.</p>
<p>Normal.</p>
<p>The kind of normal I never used to think about.</p>
<p>We ate in silence for a while.</p>
<p>Not uncomfortable.</p>
<p>Just… lived-in.</p>
<p>Like this was how life was always supposed to be.</p>
<p>At some point, Sora kicked my foot lightly under the table.</p>
<p>“What?”</p>
<p>“Nothing.”</p>
<p>“You kicked me.”</p>
<p>“I didn’t.”</p>
<p>“You did.”</p>
<p>“Prove it.”</p>
<p>I didn’t respond.</p>
<p>Because there was nothing to prove.</p>
<p>We both knew.</p>
<p>Later that day, we went out together.</p>
<p>Just walking.</p>
<p>No destination.</p>
<p>The city hadn’t changed much.</p>
<p>But I had.</p>
<p>People passed us without looking twice.</p>
<p>A couple arguing near a café.</p>
<p>Someone laughing on their phone.</p>
<p>Life moving normally around us.</p>
<p>Sora slipped her hand into mine without saying anything.</p>
<p>I looked at her.</p>
<p>She didn’t look back.</p>
<p>Like it was the most obvious thing in the world.</p>
<p>I tightened my grip slightly.</p>
<p>That was enough.</p>
<p>We stopped near a small park bench when her phone buzzed.</p>
<p>She checked it.</p>
<p>And for a moment… her expression changed.</p>
<p>Not fear.</p>
<p>Not surprise.</p>
<p>Something quieter.</p>
<p>“...Haru.”</p>
<p>“What is it?”</p>
<p>She turned the screen toward me.</p>
<p>A name I hadn’t heard in a long time.</p>
<p>Aoi.</p>
<p>I didn’t move for a second.</p>
<p>Just stared.</p>
<p>Sora watched me carefully.</p>
<p>Waiting.</p>
<p>I exhaled slowly.</p>
<p>“Is she here?”</p>
<p>“She’s asking to meet.”</p>
<p>Silence.</p>
<p>The wind moved through the trees above us.</p>
<p>It felt like the world had gotten quieter without asking permission.</p>
<p>I stood up.</p>
<p>Sora stood up with me immediately.</p>
<p>“No,” I said.</p>
<p>She paused.</p>
<p>Then I shook my head slightly.</p>
<p>“I mean… we’ll go.”</p>
<p>She studied my face.</p>
<p>“You don’t have to.”</p>
<p>“I know.”</p>
<p>Another pause.</p>
<p>Then I looked at her.</p>
<p>“I’m fine.”</p>
<p>Sora didn’t answer right away.</p>
<p>Then she squeezed my hand once.</p>
<p>Not tighter.</p>
<p>Just enough.</p>
<p>Like a reminder.</p>
<p>We found her near the station.</p>
<p>Aoi stood there like she wasn’t sure she was allowed to.</p>
<p>When she saw us, she froze.</p>
<p>“Haru…”</p>
<p>Her voice cracked slightly.</p>
<p>Sora didn’t let go of my hand.</p>
<p>She just stood beside me.</p>
<p>Quiet.</p>
<p>Present.</p>
<p>Aoi bowed her head almost immediately.</p>
<p>“I’m sorry.”</p>
<p>The words came fast.</p>
<p>Too fast.</p>
<p>Like she had been holding them in for years.</p>
<p>“I’m sorry for leaving. I’m sorry for everything. I didn’t know what to do back then, I—”</p>
<p>She stopped.</p>
<p>Her breathing shook.</p>
<p>“I messed everything up.”</p>
<p>Silence again.</p>
<p>Longer this time.</p>
<p>I looked at her.</p>
<p>And I realized something strange.</p>
<p>I wasn’t angry.</p>
<p>I wasn’t even hurt anymore.</p>
<p>It felt distant.</p>
<p>Like something that had happened to someone else.</p>
<p>“I know,” I said quietly.</p>
<p>Aoi flinched.</p>
<p>I shook my head slightly.</p>
<p>“I don’t hate you.”</p>
<p>That made her look up.</p>
<p>“But I’m not… there anymore.”</p>
<p>Her eyes widened a little.</p>
<p>Like she understood before I finished.</p>
<p>“I’ve moved on.”</p>
<p>Another pause.</p>
<p>Sora’s hand tightened just slightly in mine.</p>
<p>Not possessive.</p>
<p>Just there.</p>
<p>Aoi looked between us.</p>
<p>Really looked.</p>
<p>And something in her expression shifted.</p>
<p>Not jealousy.</p>
<p>Not regret anymore.</p>
<p>Just understanding.</p>
<p>“…I see,” she whispered.</p>
<p>Her shoulders dropped a little.</p>
<p>Like she had finally put something down she had been carrying for too long.</p>
<p>“I’m glad,” she said softly. “That you’re okay.”</p>
<p>It wasn’t a plea anymore.</p>
<p>Just honesty.</p>
<p>Sora gave a small nod.</p>
<p>Nothing dramatic.</p>
<p>Just acknowledgment.</p>
<p>Aoi stepped back.</p>
<p>Then again.</p>
<p>And then she turned away.</p>
<p>“I won’t bother you again.”</p>
<p>This time, there was no collapse in her voice.</p>
<p>Only acceptance.</p>
<p>We watched her leave.</p>
<p>Until she disappeared into the crowd.</p>
<p>For a while, neither of us moved.</p>
<p>The station noise returned slowly, like the world remembered how to breathe.</p>
<p>Sora finally spoke.</p>
<p>“That went better than I thought.”</p>
<p>“Yeah.”</p>
<p>She glanced at me.</p>
<p>“You okay?”</p>
<p>I thought about it.</p>
<p>Really thought about it.</p>
<p>Then I looked at her.</p>
<p>“I’m fine.”</p>
<p>A pause.</p>
<p>Then I added:</p>
<p>“Because I’ve got you.”</p>
<p>Sora didn’t answer immediately.</p>
<p>Then she leaned into me slightly, just enough that I could feel her there.</p>
<p>“…Good.”</p>
<p>We started walking home.</p>
<p>Hand in hand.</p>
<p>And for the first time, there was nothing left chasing either of us.</p>
